## Design Context

Captured from the approved project-site direction and conversation, 2026-10-12.

### Users

Interested engineers and technically curious readers arriving from the project
repository or a shared link. They want to see the renderer, understand the GPU
questions behind it, read the milestone findings, and inspect or run the code.

### Brand Personality

Curious, precise, personal. Andrew explains decisions and observations in his
own voice. The image invites a closer look; the writing rewards it with substance.

### Aesthetic Direction

Charlotte and Cradle are the user-selected references. Keep the existing
screenprint illustration, navy framing, ivory reading surfaces, and restrained
gold accents. Retain Cradle's self-hosted Plex typography for family continuity;
this established direction takes precedence over generic skill font exclusions.
Use actual, unaltered renderer output for evidence. Keep the artwork distinct
from the footage. Public status is milestone 1; ReSTIR is branch work in progress.

Refined 2026-10-12: match the current Cradle and Plainsight Systems site styles,
including full-width navy headers and footers. Shared colors are navy #0e1524,
ivory #fafaf5, light text #f1ebdc, and brass #d4ad5a. Use Plex Serif wordmarks
and body text, Plex Mono uppercase navigation, and a clear parent-project link.
Project artwork and content composition provide Serenity's own identity.
Plainsight Systems is the hub. Keep sibling-project discovery there; Serenity's
About page links to the hub, with no "Other work" list on the project site.
Use Cradle's exact footer structure, attribution, licenses, and typography.
Apply `andrew-voice` to public prose, including headings and introductions.
Use a single fluid content width with Cradle's responsive side gutters
(`clamp(1.25rem, 4vw, 4rem)`). Header, footer, media, and About content align;
standalone About prose fills that width, without an extra centered narrow column.

### Design Principles

1. Show the rendering and explain why the kernel work matters to inference.
2. Use generous editorial composition, readable prose, and few clear actions.
3. Label historical results and offline footage with their actual conditions.
4. Preserve full-quality media; offer delivery options without lowering the work.
5. Work on narrow screens, with keyboard navigation and reduced motion.
