Serenity

A study in light
and computation.

I’m exploring GPU performance from the rendering side: how to process data in small pieces, carry less state, and reuse work. The same questions turn up in transformer inference, and the moving lights give me a way to see what the kernel work makes possible.

03 / The question underneath

What does rendering
teach us about inference?

A renderer has more possible light paths than it can afford to trace. An inference engine has more intermediate data than it wants to move through memory. Both make you think carefully about what to keep, what to combine, and when to reuse earlier work.

Those connections run through the pipeline. Reservoirs and attention kernels combine work while carrying a small amount of state. A KV cache retains earlier inference results; ReSTIR retains useful paths. Both make the cost of storing and reading that work part of the next calculation.

Serenity now runs a learned model inside a frame fast enough to watch, on two GPUs. Moving it to the RTX 3090 Ti meant choosing which instructions to use, how to arrange their inputs and what to keep on chip, the same questions inference kernels raise. The reuse steps, which merge reservoirs much as attention merges partial softmax results, are next, tuned as kernels on each GPU Serenity runs on.
