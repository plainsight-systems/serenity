# Multiple importance sampling in the path tracer

*2026-10-11. Apple M3 Max, macOS 26.6.2, Xcode 26.2, Metal compiler
32023.864, `-std=metal4.0 -fmetal-math-mode=fast -O2`, release builds.
"Before" is `restir-di` at 57fc3da; "after" is branch `path-mis`, its
design at ffcc5bc and its implementation in the commit that adds this note.
`scenes/marbles.toml`; `graphs/path.toml` ("path") and
`graphs/restir_ris.toml` ("RIS", M = 8, visibility reuse, no merges).*

## The change, and why

The user decided on 2026-10-11, from the diagnosis of the 126,002 sample
(2026-10-11-restir-ris-results.md), that the path tracer weighs next event
estimation (NEE) against BSDF sampling by multiple importance sampling (MIS),
as pbrt-v4's PathIntegrator and Falcor do. Light sampling alone at the
marbles' near-mirror steel (GGX α = 10^-3) returns rare samples of 10^5,
unbiased, with variance growing as 1/α². Two decisions change with it
(`docs/process/MEMORY.md`, 2026-10-11): principle 7's one-way counting
(`logical-overview.md`), and the time baseline as "the textbook kernel".

What the path tracer does now (`src/metal/integrator/path.metal.h`):

- **Step 5, NEE**: as before, then weighted by
  w_l = (P p_l)² / ((P p_l)² + p_b²), p_b the BSDF's `pdf()` of the
  direction. The term is computed whole before the shadow ray.
- **Step 3, a light struck by a bounced ray**: counted with
  w_b = p_b² / (p_b² + (P p_l')²) after a lobe that is not delta, p_b the
  pdf the bounce was drawn with and p_l' contract 3's `light_pdf()` asked
  from the ray's own origin; in full after a delta lobe and on the camera's
  ray.
- **The sky** is counted one way, as before; **roulette, eta scale and media**
  are unchanged.
- **ReSTIR's first surface q**: its direct light stays the reservoir's
  alone; the bounce from q counts no light it strikes off a non-delta lobe
  (`not_counted`), a delta lobe's in full. Past q the continuation is the
  path tracer's loop, MIS and all.
- **What the loop carries**: one float, `emission_pdf`, in place of the
  bool it replaces. No ray and no random number is added.
- **`light_probability()`** in `uniform_light.metal.h`, the selection's 1/N.

## A bias found, and its fix: where p_l is asked

The first implementation asked step 3's p_l' from the surface's own point,
`from`, the point step 5 draws from. Its convergence tables looked right
(MSE at 1024 samples 32 times lower than before), and its image mean at
t = 120 was +0.48% over the reference. Four more independent 1024-sample
batches gave 0.18725 to 0.18731 against the base's 0.18612 to 0.18637 and
the reference's 0.18637: systematic, not noise. Summed over 60 x 60 blocks,
the first version was 2.6% brighter than the reference in the median block
(the base: 0.04% darker), +3% over the whole table and 0% on the fireflies
seen directly.

Isolated with diagnostic builds on a Lambert floor under two fireflies of
radius 3 mm, one 2 cm over the floor (1024 samples, against the base):

| Build | What differs from the first version | Floor mean / base |
|---|---|---|
| first version | | 1.0068 |
| C | step 3 off non-delta lobes as before MIS (not counted) | 0.9999 |
| D | step 5 unweighted (w_l = 1) | 1.0069 |
| G | step 3's weight 0 where `light_pdf()` is 0, not 1 | 1.0000 |

Against the closed form, albedo L sin² cos summed over the lights at 4 x 4
points a pixel, the base was within 0.1% everywhere and the first version
+1.5 to +4.5% by block.

The cause: a bounced ray starts `ray_offset`, 0.1 mm, off the surface. For a
firefly 3 mm across, that is 3% of its width, so the cone the ray's origin
sees is shifted from the cone step 5 draws from at the point. A light the
ray struck in the crescent only its own cone holds had `light_pdf()` 0 from
the point, so w_b = 1, counted in full beside NEE's whole cone. The design's
header had called this "a sliver"; at the marbles' scale it is not.

The fix asks p_l' from the ray's origin, `along.origin`, which the loop
already carries. The crescent the ray alone sees is then weighed as the
crescent NEE alone draws, on the cone's other side, and the two cancel to
first order. On the same floor the fixed build equals the base to 1 part in
10^6. On the marbles at t = 120 its median block is 1.0002 of the
reference's. A test now holds it: a 3 mm firefly 2 cm over a floor against
the closed form to 0.5% (`path_test.cpp`); with step 3 asked from the
point, it fails by +1.4 to +5.6%.

- **The overflow test's scene.** `blinding_lights()` was two lights at the

Facts:

- B is 0.11 ms slower than MIS: computing step 5's term before its query
  holds less across it. Rejected; MIS keeps the term before the query.
- F computes what the base computes, with only `emission_pdf` (a float
  carried across every trace) in place of the bool. It costs 0.50 ms at
  t = 120 and 0.37 at t = 0.5: most of MIS's cost.
- The weights themselves: step 5's 0.12 to 0.16 ms (MIS − D), step 3's
  0.04 to 0.11 (MIS − C).

Inference: the path kernel's occupancy is limited by per-thread state
(2026-10-10-path-kernel-counters.md), and one more 32-bit value live across
every ray query moves it. The bool it replaces cost less, perhaps held in a
predicate. No counters were recorded to confirm this.

Not tried: storing the density in fewer bits. A GGX density at α = 10^-3
reaches 10^8, past a half's range; a logarithm or a shared exponent would
fit, at the cost of a conversion each surface and a weight no longer exact.

### One origin

Every ray leaves a surface from `leaving_point()`, `ray_offset` (0.1 mm) off
it on the side it leaves toward (`integrator/surface.metal.h`, now a function
`leave()` calls, beside `leave_alike()`). The fix:

- **Step 5** draws its light sample from `next_event_origin()`, the leaving
  point on wo's side; its density P p_l is the cone's from there; its shadow
  ray starts there (`ray(origin, wi, 0, distance)`, not `leave()` from the
  point); a drawn direction that leaves on wo's other side it does not take.
  Every lobe that is not delta reflects (Lambert, conductor, the coated
  base), so f is 0 there and nothing is lost. T is measured from the
  surface's point to the light's, as step 1 measures a stretch.
- **Step 6** hands on `handed_on()`: counted_in_full for a delta lobe, and
  for a direction on wo's other side, which step 5 does not draw (w_b = 1);
  else the density it drew with.
- **Step 3** asks p_l from the bounce's own origin, as 7751a65 did.

The proof that w_l + w_b = 1, against the points the shader uses. Take a
direction wi both strategies can draw at a surface with wo:

1. Step 5 draws only directions that leave alike with wo, so wi does, and
   step 6 hands on its density b' = the pdf `sample()` drew it with.
2. Step 5 draws from `leaving_point(surface, wo)`; the bounce along wi leaves
   from `leaving_point(surface, wi)` (`leave()`, step 8), which step 3 asks
   p_l from. Both are `position + ray_offset * (leaves_outward(d) ? n : -n)`
   on the same position and normal, and `leaves_outward` gives the same
   answer for wo and wi (1): the same bits.
3. Step 5's density is `select_light()`'s 1/N times `sample_light()`'s pdf
   from that point; step 3's is `light_probability()`'s 1/N times
   `light_pdf()` from that point. Both pdfs are 1 over the solid angle
   `view_light()` gives for the same light at the same point: one a.
4. Step 5's BSDF density is `pdf(wo, wi)` = b; step 3's is b', equal to b up
   to the rounding of a conductor's half vector found two ways.
5. w_l = a^2 / (a^2 + b^2), w_b = b'^2 / (b'^2 + a^2): with the ratio form,
   r = min/max of the same pair, w_l = 1 / (1 + r^2) and w_b = r^2 / (1 + r^2)
   (or the reverse): the sum is 1 to rounding.
6. A direction only one strategy draws has the other's density 0: outside
   the cone from that point `light_pdf()` is 0 and w_b = 1; on wo's other side
   step 6 hands on counted_in_full and w_b = 1.

7751a65 drew step 5 from `position` and asked step 3 from
`position + ray_offset n`: two values of a for one direction, and in the
crescents of the light that one point's cone holds and the other's does
not, one of them 0.

The probe (`mis_weights`, `kernels/path_probe.metal`) now asks every density
from the shader's own functions at the shader's own points
(`next_event_origin()`, `leave().origin`, `handed_on()`), at a surface with a
geometric normal, under two fireflies: 0.3 across at 1.6 (as before) and
3 mm across at 2 cm along the mirror direction (new), each at a conductor
(alpha 0.1) and a Lambert surface, 4096 draws of each strategy:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.3 at 1.6 | conductor | 6449 | 1.0e-5 | 0 |
| 0.3 at 1.6 | Lambert | 4163 | 9.7e-8 | 0 |
| 3 mm at 2 cm | conductor | 6048 | 1.6e-5 | 0 |
| 3 mm at 2 cm | Lambert | 4141 | 9.5e-8 | 0 |

### What the image now estimates

The light as seen from the point the rays leave from, 0.1 mm off the
surface: the integrand both strategies share, as the bounce always saw it.
For a light at height h that is a relative 2 ray_offset / h more directly
below it and somewhat less to its side. pbrt-v4's PathIntegrator does the
same: its `SampleLd()` moves the light sample's reference point to the ray
origin offset toward wo for a reflective BSDF, while its BSDF-hit PDF is
asked from the previous intersection, its offset the float error's bound,
so the difference is below its noise.

closed form averaged over 4 x 4 points of it (Lambert: albedo L sin² cos;
glossy: the probe's midpoint integral over the cone at 128 x 128):

**Lambert floor, firefly 3 mm across 2 cm over it** (the 0.5% test's

### The power heuristic without overflow

`power_heuristic(f, g)` is now computed from r = min(f, g) / max(f, g):
1 / (1 + r²) for the larger's strategy, r² / (1 + r²) for the smaller's.
No density is squared. Equal densities, both infinite included, give 1/2;
g = 0 gives 1; an infinite density beside a finite one takes the whole
weight (a finite number over infinity is 0, under fast math as well). The
`< 10^19` precondition is gone: the scene accepts any finite radius above
0, so it could not be promised.

A new probe kernel (`heuristic_weights`) asks it of 14 pairs, among them
3.2 x 10^19 beside 0.3 (a 10^-10 m light seen from 1 m), 10^20 beside
10^20, the largest float, infinities and 0: every weight finite, within
10^-6 of f²/(f² + g²) in doubles (its limits at infinity), and the two ways
round summing to 1. The probe also computes 7751a65's squared form as a
control: NaN at 9 of the 14 pairs. And end to end: a sphere light of
radius 10^-10 m and radiance 10^20, 2.5 m over a floor, converges to its
closed form with no sample non-finite (density some 2 x 10^20).

### ReSTIR DI

The light functions (`sample_light()`, `light_pdf()`, contract 4's point
draws) are unchanged; only `path.metal.h` and `leave()`'s factoring changed.
What changes for ReSTIR:

- **Its continuation** (`continue_path()`, past q) is the path tracer's loop,
  so its step 5 at later surfaces now draws from the leaving point, and its
  step 6 hands on by `handed_on()`.
- **At q**, the bounce's light is not counted for any lobe that is not
  delta, decided by the lobe drawn (`walk_path()` now returns it from step 6
  to 8); the same answer as 7751a65's test of emission_pdf for every lobe
  the scenes have.
- **Its first surface's candidates and visibility** are unchanged: they draw
  from the surface record's point and leave by `leave()`, which is the same
  expression it was. MIS does not weigh them (Phase B's BSDF candidate
  will), so there is no pair of densities to keep consistent there; whether
  their target should also be seen from the leaving point is Phase B's
  question.

The ReSTIR tests (`restir_test.cpp`) pass unchanged; the RIS graph's frame

### The claims corrected

- **Device-memory reads.** The header now counts what MIS reads, per frame of
  N pixels with H_nd surfaces reached after a lobe that is not delta: 4 N H_nd
  logical bytes of `shape_lights[primitive]` (constant address space, cached
  in the common case; not measured apart), and on a light hit the light's
  record, sphere, transform and glow (76 bytes), the same addresses step 3's
  L_e already reads (GDSA.6). The performance review's figure: 30.9 MB per
  average such surface at 3456 x 2234.
- **Roulette.** Its survival q is found from beta after step 6, so it does
  depend on the direction drawn. It enters no MIS weight because it is
  conditionally unbiased: given the direction, the path goes on with chance q
  and its beta is divided by q, so what the path goes on to gather has the
  expectation it had without roulette, whatever q depends on.

### Open

- **The light seen from 0.1 mm up.** Consistent weights estimate the light
  as seen from the leaving point. With `ray_offset` a fixed 0.1 mm and
  fireflies 1.5 mm in radius resting on surfaces, that is visible: 1 to 2%
  in some blocks at t = 8 against both references, where 7751a65's step 5,
  drawn from the point, carried the light as seen from the point. Not
  MIS's: the bounce has always seen the light so; MIS made both strategies
  see it the same way. The cure is the offset's, for every estimator: an
  origin offset scaled to the float error of the point (pbrt-v4's
  `OffsetRayOrigin()`, its error bounds), or rays traced from the point
  itself with a minimum distance. A change of `leave()`, to be measured on
  its own (self-intersection at grazing angles, the medium stretch of
  step 1, ReSTIR's visibility), not done here.
- **The references.** The MIS references in `.cache/references` were
  rendered by 7751a65, whose step 5 drew from the point; at t = 8 the fix's
  median block is 0.9986 of them. The references before MIS, from an
  independent estimator, agree with that (0.9989). If the offset stays as
  it is, references rendered by this build would be the ones to measure
  against; if it changes, after that change.
- **Not reachable by a test:** `handed_on()`'s counted_in_full for a
  direction a non-delta lobe sends to wo's other side, and ReSTIR's q
  decided by the lobe drawn. Both matter only for a direction rounding puts
  just past the horizon until a material has a rough transmission lobe.
- **Counters** for the register inference in the frame-time diagnosis.
